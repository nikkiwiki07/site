Somewhere Out There, a Brain Is Assembling Itself From Random Heat, and Statistically It's More "You" Than You Are

Run any universe long enough and thermodynamics gets bored. Given enough time and enough empty space, random fluctuations in a high-entropy vacuum should — purely by chance — occasionally assemble a fully-formed, momentarily conscious brain, complete with false memories of a life it never lived. Cosmologists call these Boltzmann brains, and the unsettling part isn't that they might exist. It's that in most eternally-inflating models of the cosmos, they should vastly outnumber brains like yours, built the slow, expensive way through stars, planets, and evolution.

If a theory predicts you're overwhelmingly more likely to be a stray fluctuation than a real observer, something in the theory is probably broken — which is exactly why physicists keep this one filed under "informative failure" rather than "prediction." It's less a threat to reality and more a stress test for our models of it.

Filed under: cosmology's favorite way of finding out its own equations have gone somewhere it didn't intend.

08 ANOMALIES · STATUS MIXED
FILE 0119-W

The Wow! Signal

In 1977, a radio telescope listening for extraterrestrial hydrogen chatter caught 72 seconds of a narrowband signal 30 times louder than background noise, at almost exactly the frequency theorists said an alien broadcast should use. It was never seen again, on that frequency or any other, despite decades of looking.

UNEXPLAINED
FILE 0044-B

The Boötes Void

A roughly spherical pocket of space 330 million light-years across containing a few dozen galaxies where tens of thousands were expected. Early astronomers half-joked you could fit the entire Milky Way's local neighborhood inside it sixty times over and lose it.

STRUCTURAL OUTLIER
FILE 0077-G

The Great Attractor

Our entire Local Group of galaxies is being pulled sideways at roughly 600 km/s toward a gravitational mass we can't directly see, because it sits behind the dust and stars of our own galactic plane. We've named the blind spot. We haven't solved it.

ACTIVELY PULLING
FILE 0231-P

Planet Nine

A cluster of distant icy objects beyond Neptune all tilt and align their orbits in a way that's hard to explain by chance. The tidy fix is an undiscovered planet several times Earth's mass, quietly shepherding them from the dark. Nobody has photographed it. Yet.

UNCONFIRMED
FILE 0182-O

'Oumuamua

The first confirmed visitor from beyond our solar system: a strange, elongated object that sped up slightly as it left, in a way comets usually explain with jets of gas. No jets were seen. The leading theory is exotic ice. The tabloid theory is considerably more exciting.

DEPARTED, UNEXPLAINED
FILE 0056-C

The CMB Cold Spot

A patch of the cosmic microwave background — the afterglow of the Big Bang itself — that's colder than it has any right to be. The boring explanation is an enormous supervoid. The fun explanation, floated in some cyclic models, is a bruise left by contact with another universe.

UNDER OBSERVATION
FILE 0403-D

Dark Flow

Vast clusters of galaxies appear to be streaming together in one coordinated direction, at a speed and heading that nothing in the visible universe accounts for. One reading: they're being tugged by mass structures that sit beyond the edge of what we can ever observe.

DIRECTION UNKNOWN
FILE 0128-T

Tabby's Star

KIC 8462852 dims irregularly and dramatically — by as much as 22% at a time, with no repeating pattern. It briefly became astronomy's most serious flirtation with the phrase "alien megastructure" before uneven clouds of dust took over as the leading, less thrilling explanation.
